# AutoFix Workshop - Executive Summary

## Purpose
This document summarizes the security review completed for AutoFix Workshop. The goal was to understand what technology the shop relies on, identify what could go wrong, and put simple rules in place to reduce risk - all in plain, practical terms.

## What We Looked At
We built a full inventory of the shop's IT assets, covering three areas:
- **Hardware** - computers, diagnostic scanners, the card reader, Wi-Fi router, and backup drive
- **Software** - the booking app, QuickBooks, antivirus, and other programs staff use daily
- **Data** - where customer information, payment records, and employee details are stored

## Top Risks Identified
Based on that inventory, five key risks stood out:
1. **Ransomware** hitting the back office laptop, which holds financial and payroll data
2. **Unauthorized access** to customer contact information
3. **Card skimming** at the point of sale
4. **Network intrusion** through the guest Wi-Fi
5. **Service outages** affecting the booking app or QuickBooks

Each of these was scored by how likely it is and how much damage it could cause, so the most serious risks are clear at a glance.

## What We Put in Place
To reduce these risks, two policies were written for staff:
- An **Acceptable Use Policy**, covering how computers and shared terminals should be used day to day
- A **Password and Access Control Policy**, covering password rules, screen-locking, and who can access sensitive systems

Both policies are written in plain language so any staff member - mechanic or front-desk - can follow them without needing technical knowledge.

## Why This Matters
None of this requires expensive tools or a dedicated IT person. It's a set of low-cost, practical habits that meaningfully reduce the shop's exposure to the most common risks facing a small business like AutoFix.
